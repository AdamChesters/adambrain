Adambrain

An interactive portrait of my brain,
built from MRI scans.

Adam Chesters3D / MRI / Browser
Explore the volume ↗GitHub repository ↗

Rotate the model, look beneath the surface, and change the opacity and colour of each tissue layer. The opening demo gently fades skin and bone away, pauses to show what’s underneath, then brings them back.

Use Start / Stop to control the motion and Default view to return to the starting point. Save and Load keep a favourite view in your browser.

This is a scan-derived artwork. Some tissue boundaries and vessel classifications are interpretations; the viewer’s Data tab explains how the model was made.

ABOUT THE PROJECT

From scan slices to a portrait.

The source archive contains 15,191 DICOM MRI images from two studies, head and spine, organised into 133 image series. Every series was inventoried and reviewed using representative images to see what it could contribute. This portrait uses the head study, acquired on a Siemens MAGNETOM Sola 1.5 T MRI scanner.

A lot of those images show the same anatomy at different times or from different angles. They aren’t all separate scans with new detail. For example, the angiography sequence contains 40 original volumes and 39 background-subtracted volumes following contrast through the vessels.

The images we used

  • T1 MPRAGE, before and after contrast: two 208-slice volumes provide the main head and brain reference, with approximately 1 mm stored spacing.
  • TWIST contrast angiography: the first nine subtracted phases provide the main vessel signal. All 39 subtracted phases were reviewed; their timing helps estimate arterial and venous groups.
  • T2, FLAIR and susceptibility-weighted images: complementary views help cross-check brain, fluid and vessel boundaries.
  • Targeted eye scans, including STIR and Dixon-water: supporting views help estimate the eye outlines.

How it comes together

The slices are ordered by their recorded position and assembled into 3D volumes. Compatible head scans are aligned in the same physical space, then compared to estimate the tissue layers. Vessel signal is combined across the early contrast phases; different MRI contrasts support different structures, rather than being blindly added together.

The resulting volumes are compressed for the browser. The viewer samples through them to build the image you see, with separate colour and opacity controls. FreeSurfer helps estimate brain tissues; these boundaries and the vessel labels remain interpretations of the scans.

What’s being worked on

A more performant rendering method is being worked on, with the aim of keeping fine detail while making the viewer smoother on phones and less powerful computers. Other work focuses on fine vessel continuity, tissue boundaries and loading performance.

OPEN SOURCE

Credits

This project builds on the work of these open source communities.

Three.js
Browser rendering and camera controls.
dcm2niix
Converting selected DICOM stacks into usable 3D volumes.
pydicom and pylibjpeg
Reading the DICOM archive and checking decoded scan values.
FreeSurfer
Brain reconstruction and tissue estimates.
3D Slicer
Source-linked inspection and editable image and tissue layers.
SimpleITK
Image alignment and processing.
NumPy, SciPy and NiBabel
Volume calculations, filtering and neuroimaging file handling.
scikit-image and Pillow
Image processing, surface experiments and review images.
Earlier prototypes and reconstruction experiments

brain2print and NiiVue helped with early browser-based tissue previews. Blender was used for editable presentation models.

Offline reconstruction experiments used PyTorch, NiftyMIC, NeSVoR, UniRes and Spinal Cord Toolbox. Those experiments aren’t all part of this head viewer.
